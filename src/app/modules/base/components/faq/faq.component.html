Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is a systematic review?

A systematic review is a structured, comprehensive approach to identifying, evaluating, and synthesizing all relevant studies on a particular research topic. It follows a transparent methodology, often guided by frameworks like PRISMA, to minimize bias and ensure reproducibility.

Why do we combine "title" and "abstract" into a single "Record"?

Combining the title and abstract columns into a single record ensures that the LLM has a more complete view of each article’s essential information, streamlining both the screening and embedding processes.

What is the difference between “Screening 1” and “Screening 2”?

Screening 1 refers to the initial step of title and abstract screening to quickly filter out irrelevant or low-quality articles. Screening 2 is the subsequent full-text review stage where remaining studies are evaluated in detail to confirm their inclusion or exclusion in the review.

Why embed the documents with a vector database like Chroma?

Using Chroma for vector embeddings allows us to efficiently retrieve and filter relevant records for each screening step. Its built-in filtering ensures precise document retrieval, minimizing the risk of cross-contamination between articles and preserving data integrity during automated screening.

What are the three reviews (Physio, Neuro, DigiHealth) about?

Review I (Physio): Focuses on economic evaluations of musculoskeletal physiotherapy.
Review II (Neuro): Examines effectiveness of pharmacological and gene-based treatments in hereditary peripheral neuropathies.
Review III (DigiHealth): Assesses the cost-effectiveness of AI applications within healthcare settings.

How do you handle non-English or duplicate records?

Non-English or duplicate records are excluded during the preprocessing stage, ensuring that only unique and English-language articles remain in the final dataset for screening and analysis.
